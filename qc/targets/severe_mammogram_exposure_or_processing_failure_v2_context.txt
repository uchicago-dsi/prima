Decide whether the visual target "severe mammogram exposure or processing failure" is present in the large panel labeled TARGET VIEW.

The three smaller panels are SAME-EXAM REFERENCES. Classify only the TARGET VIEW. The references are comparison aids, not labels: a reference may itself be unusable, and a target does not become positive merely because it differs from a reference. Never average the panels into one exam-level decision.

Answer YES only when the TARGET VIEW itself has unmistakable loss of clinically meaningful image texture across a substantial breast region. Positive appearances include a broad target region that is nearly featureless white or black, severe saturation or clipping, a large non-anatomic intensity collapse, or gross processing corruption that erases internal tissue structure. Similar loss in another same-side projection, together with preserved tissue texture in the opposite breast, can support the judgment when the loss is directly visible in the target.

Pathology, skin folds, scars, implants, devices, digitized-film appearance, detector seams, cropping, positioning, and compression hardware are separate targets and do not establish this target. If only a SAME-EXAM REFERENCE has severe feature loss, answer NO for the target.
