Answer YES when the pixels show a deliberate mammographic magnification acquisition rather than an ordinary full-field CC or MLO image. Check independently for these visible patterns:

- a magnification platform, raised support, air-gap geometry, frame, rail, or non-standard receptor boundary;
- a square or rectangular diagnostic field bounded on multiple sides, with breast structures visibly enlarged inside it;
- a large-field magnification view that contains substantial breast tissue but has enlarged anatomy and non-standard field geometry compared with an ordinary whole-breast acquisition.

Magnification hardware can fall outside the rendered crop, so a visible platform is not required. A large rectangular field plus convincingly enlarged tissue can be sufficient. Burned-in projection text containing M may support matching visual geometry, but text alone is never sufficient.

Answer NO for an ordinary full-field CC or MLO acquisition, a spot-compression-only image with a focal paddle but no magnification evidence, an implant or implant-displaced view, simple cropping, a single detector edge, or digitized-film borders without enlarged magnification geometry. Clips, wires, skin markers, localization hardware, text, image noise, and an old-film appearance do not by themselves establish magnification.

Judge the acquisition geometry rather than whether a lesion looks large. Use high confidence when magnification geometry or the combination of a bounded diagnostic field and enlarged anatomy is convincing.
